This is your homepage, and everything on it is a placeholder. Replace it with
whatever you'd want a person to read first — a colleague looking you up before a
conference, a student deciding whether to email you, someone reviewing a grant.

Two or three short paragraphs is plenty. A wall of text on a homepage goes unread.

## What I work on

Say what you actually study, in language a scientist outside your subfield could
follow. This is the paragraph people skim to decide whether you're relevant to them,
so lead with the question rather than the technique.

If it helps, a structure that works: the broad problem, the specific thing you do
about it, and why the answer would matter.

## Current projects

A short list works well here — a line or two each, rather than an abstract. Link to
the papers, the code, or the data if they're public. If a project has a DOI, use it
instead of a bare URL, because a DOI keeps pointing at the right thing after the
repository moves.

## Elsewhere

The icons in the sidebar come from `_config.yml`. Fill in the ones you use and leave
the rest blank — they only appear if you give them a value.

### Editing this site

Every page lives in the `_pages` folder and is written in Markdown, the same
plain-text formatting you may already use in R Markdown or Quarto. Click any file on
GitHub, click the pencil icon, make a change, and click *Commit changes*. Your site
rebuilds itself within a minute or two.

The other folders hold the sections linked in the menu at the top:

- `_publications` — one file per paper
- `_talks` — one file per talk
- `_teaching` — one file per course
- `_posts` — blog posts, if you want them
- `_portfolio` — projects

Each currently holds a single example file. Copy its shape, or delete it if you don't
want that section. Sections are removed from the top menu in `_data/navigation.yml`.

There's a much fuller guide at <https://academicpages.github.io>, the project this
template is built on.
